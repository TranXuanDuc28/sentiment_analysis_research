Transfer Learning
Transfer learning isa machine learning technique in which knowledge gained through one task or dataset is used to improve model performance on another related task and/or different dataset. In other words, transfer learning uses what has been learned in one setting to improve generalization in another setting.
Transfer learning uses pre-trained models from one machine learning task or dataset to improve performance and generalizability on a related task or dataset.For example, for image classification, knowledge gained while learning to recognize dogs could be applied when trying to recognize cats.

Domain Adaptation
Adapting a model that was trained on a source domain to a target domain, where the data distribution may differ (in the same problem)
For example:The source domain : movie reviews.The target domain : product reviews.
The data distribution may vary in terms of vocabulary/style/tone/or sentiment polarity.
No label or lack of labels in the target domain.
--- PAGE 19 ---
Domain Adaptation•Adjust models to work well in specific domains.•Improve the model's performance on new domains without retraining from scratch 

--- PAGE 20 ---
Domain Adaptation: Important•Improving the performance •Generalizing the model on new domains without requiring a lot of labeled data from the target domain. •It addresses data scarcity by enabling models to leverage existing datasets, thus saving on the costs and efforts of collecting new labeled data.•This approach increases efficiency by reducing the need to develop new models from scratch and broadens the applicability of machine learning across various fields.•Domain adaptation ensures models remain effective and relevant in rapidly changing or privacy-sensitive environments, facilitating better predictions and decision-making in diverse applications.
--- PAGE 21 ---
Domain Adaptation: Important•save time and resources•enable to apply the model to a wider range of scenarios•make models more flexible and effective in real-world applications where data can vary•For example: Having a sentiment classifier that works well on movie reviews •can adapt it to product reviews•can use it to analyze customer feedback, market trends, or competitor analysis.

Multi-task Learning
•Train model on multiple tasks at the same time. •Ex: Multi-task learning model for sentiment analysis in single domain or multiple domains. •Improve overall model performance (information sharing between tasks)•Increase model generalization ability
--- PAGE 27 ---
Multi-task Learning: Methods
•Hard Parameter Sharing•Soft Parameter Sharing

--- PAGE 28 ---
Multi-task Learning•Goal: share knowledge between tasks to help the model generalize better and avoid overfitting.•A language processing model can both classify the sentiment and predict the topic of the same sentence.•A vision model can both recognize objects and detect locations (bounding boxes).
--- PAGE 29 ---
Hard Parameter Sharing•Most popular architectures in Multitask Learning.•Different tasks share a large part of parameters (shared layers), usually deep feature extractor layers.•Directly share parameters•Then each task has its own output layers (task-specific layers).
--- PAGE 30 ---
shared_encoder = TransformerEncoder()sentiment_head = nn.Linear(768, 3)topic_head = nn.Linear(768, 5)
--- PAGE 31 ---
•Pros•Reduce overfitting•Tasks force model learn common feature•Avoid depend too much on specific noise of each task.•Save resources•Share most parameters •Reduce the number of weights, save memory & training time.•Increase generalization ability•When tasks are related, joint learning helps the model understand the data more deeply.
--- PAGE 32 ---
•Cons•Negative transfer: When tasks are not really related, sharing parameters can reduce the efficiency of each task.•Inflexible: Difficult to adapt when a task requires a significantly different architecture or number of parameters.
--- PAGE 33 ---
Soft Parameter Sharing•Each task has its own model (do not share layers)•But the parameters of these models are constrained to not differ too much through regularization.•Each task has its own architecture, but forces them to learn similar parameters.•Constrain the similarity between parameters.
--- PAGE 34 ---
Multi-task Learning: Implementation Process•Data Preparation•Collect data•Prepare data for multiple tasks•Model design•Designing multitask learning model•Model Training •Train model on different tasks simultaneously•Evaluation and improvement•Evaluate performance on each task•Fine-tuning the model for best performance on all tasks

Multilingual Word Embeddings 
multilingual word representations in vector spaceallowing words from different languages to be mapped into the same vector spaceThis means that words with equivalent or nearly identical meanings in different languages will be represented by vectors close to each other in this space.
--- PAGE 9 ---
Multilingual Word Embeddings: properties
General semantic word mapWords with similar meanings will be represented close to each other, regardless of languageEX: "apple" in English and "manzana" in Spanish can be represented by vectors that are close to each other.
Applied in multilingual problemsmachine translationlanguage classificationmultilingual searchnatural language understanding systems that support multiple languages
--- PAGE 10 ---
Multilingual embeddings: Training methods•Translate words directly•Use a bilingual dictionary or bilingual data to map words between languages•Training with concurrent models•Train a single model with data from multiple languages to create a common vector space•Fine-tuning on additional languages•Extends a monolingual model by fine-tuning with multilingual data to preserve semantics in the vector space.
